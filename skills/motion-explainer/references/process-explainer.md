# Example: from rough draft to approved document

Create a 45-second motion-graphics explainer for a nontechnical audience showing how a rough document becomes an approved version. The main takeaway is that writing, review, and revision form a clear loop, with one approved document at the end.

Use the motion-explainer skill. Handle art direction, implementation, rendering, visual inspection, refinement, and delivery end to end. Work in a new output project outside this skill repository. Deliver a 1920×1080, 60fps MP4 and editable source with reproduction instructions.

## Story

Follow the same fictional document, titled “Weekend Plan,” throughout:

1. Start with a rough draft: title, a few short paragraphs, and visible gaps.
2. Organize its scattered notes into a clear outline.
3. Send the draft into review; attach two concise comments to specific passages.
4. Revise those passages and resolve the comments. Show this loop once.
5. End with the approved document and a separate exported PDF icon.

This is an illustrative process, not a recording of a real product. Use generated text and vector shapes; no external footage, screenshots, personal data, or product logos are needed. Do not invent performance metrics or imply the animation is a functioning document editor.

## Creative direction

Make it calm, readable, and warm. Keep the document recognizable as its contents and layout change. Use a restrained palette and one accent color for active changes. Let comments attach to text, passages rearrange, and resolved notes settle away. Keep headings short and avoid paragraphs of on-screen explanation.

An original, quiet synthesized score and a few subtle action cues are welcome. No narration or purchased services. Let the final approved state hold long enough to read.

## Review and delivery

Inspect rendered scenes and the transitions into review, revision, and approval. Check text overlap and legibility. Render the full film, verify its actual dimensions, cadence, duration, complete decode, color handling, and audio timing/levels if audio is included. Report which visual and listening reviews actually occurred, alongside the MP4, source, and concise creative notes.
